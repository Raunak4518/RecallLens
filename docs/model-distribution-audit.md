# Actual model distribution and provenance audit

Audited 18 November 2026 using local staged files, staging scripts, official model cards/repositories and publisher dataset pages. No weights or datasets were downloaded for this audit. Small license/model-card text files were staged outside the source/model verification freeze. This is an engineering provenance inventory, not a claim of complete legal clearance.

## Immediate publication gaps

1. The actual .local-models/clip directory has weights, tokenizer and preprocessing but no copyright/license file. Include OpenAI's MIT copyright/license and the pinned ONNX converter's provenance before public distribution. Exact texts and source hashes are staged in .agent-staging/model-notices. The converter card says MIT; its repository API listing has no separate license file. Do not fabricate one. [OpenAI license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/CLIP/main/LICENSE) · [Pinned converter card](https://huggingface.co/Xenova/clip-vit-base-patch32/blob/6b7c523703ae26e39be4b03774966f8571c7f523/README.md)
2. Preserve the distinction between permissive license terms and intended-use guidance. CLIP's publisher describes deployed applications as out of scope and recommends task-specific assessment. The project must report its in-domain limitations rather than treating the MIT tag as a quality/safety approval. This is not an assertion that the model card converts MIT into a noncommercial license. [CLIP model card](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/CLIP/main/model-card.md)
3. ML Kit is a licensed Google SDK/model service, not an Apache-licensed weight download. The terms restrict extraction/reverse engineering and require applicable user disclosures for diagnostic metrics; inputs/results are processed on-device. The Apache footer on the documentation covers sample code, not the SDK/model terms. [ML Kit terms](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/terms)
4. Dataset attribution needs annotation/media separation. HierText says CC BY-SA 4.0 for its dataset, while its Open Images source photos have per-image licenses. Calling every resulting fixture/annotation simply CC BY 2.0 is incomplete. [HierText license section](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/hiertext#license) · [Open Images image information](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/download_v7.html#image-information)

## Evaluation data is not a product asset license

Open Images supplies source/landing URLs, author/title, rotation and per-image license fields. Preserve those fields for each selected fixture and document crops/resizes. HierText annotations must retain their CC BY-SA4.0 notice separately; underlying image rights follow their own metadata. The existing benchmark's independent-annotation/split limitations are also quality issues, not solved by attribution. [Image metadata specification](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/download_v7.html#image-information) · [HierText publisher](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/hiertext)

COCO licenses its annotations separately from the copyrighted photographs and points users to source-image terms. Do not package arbitrary COCO examples based solely on its annotation license. Objectron's publisher applies C-UDA1.0, which needs its own compliance record; it is not CC BY merely because another dataset is. PubFig83 offers research downloads/citation guidance but the reviewed page does not provide an explicit blanket image redistribution/commercial license. Keep PubFig83 media out of public app/test packages until rights are established, or replace those cases with clearly licensed/consented identity data. Public figures and publicly downloadable photos are not equivalent to permission. [COCO terms](https://cocodataset.org/dataset/termsofuse.htm) · [Objectron license](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Objectron#license) · [PubFig83 publisher](https://vision.seas.harvard.edu/pubfig83/)
